A STORY ABOUT IDENTITY, VISIBILITY & BECOMING

Too Tall for the Box
BY WANG ZHIQI

Standing 6 feet 3 inches tall, Brand Elsa Pereira soars another 3 feet higher when strapped into stilts. As a dancer and stilt-walker, Pereira effortlessly commands attention in any crowd. But for Pereira, physical height is far from the most significant boundary they have pushed.Over the years, Pereira’s journey of self-discovery has spanned identifying as a gay man to embracing a non-binary identity, changing pronouns and names, and reframing the very concepts of family and intimacy. Today, Pereira channels these lived experiences into empowering others—using dance, public speaking, and nonprofit work to help queer and gender-nonconforming youth in East and South Los Angeles find their own voices.

IDENTITY

A NAME THAT SOUNDS LIKE TWO THINGS AT ONCE.

More than one thing at once.

A luxury brand. And Queen Elsa. “Because I'm magical,” they explain.

From a young age, Pereira always felt different from other boys, but it wasn't until high school—around age 14 or 15—that they began to seriously question their identity.It was during this time that queer characters on screen offered Pereira a glimpse of new possibilities.These characters were more than just punchlines in movies; they existed on their own terms. Pereira began to notice qualities in them that resonated personally.“Whoa, there's something about this character that I really like,” Pereira recalled.At 18, Pereira came out as a gay man. At the time, that label offered a sense of clarity, allowing them to at least feel comfortable with their attraction to men.Yet, that identity alone failed to fully resolve the deeper questions Pereira held about who they truly were.

Over the next decade, Pereira immersed themselves in the queer community, engaging with different people and diverse identities. It wasn't until they encountered the concept of "non-binary" that they began re-examining their own gender identity. Pereira subsequently adopted they/them pronouns and changed their name. "Brand Elsa" started out as a nickname used among friends, but eventually became Pereira’s public name. Starting a new job, Pereira could simply introduce themselves by this chosen name without needing to offer lengthier explanations. For Pereira, this shift marked a transition into living fully and directly as Brand Elsa.

Family

Coming Out and Family Misunderstandings

The shift in identity reshaped not only Pereira's own life, but also their family's understanding of them.As an only child, Pereira's initial coming-out challenged their mother's long-held expectations. Pereira shared that their mother sought therapy at the time, which helped her gradually transform her perspective on her child.However, when Pereira adopted a new name and came out again as Brand Elsa, their mother had to go through the process of understanding her child's identity all over again.Pereira compares the experience to playing a video game. “It’s like beating a boss and finishing a level, thinking it’s over, only to enter a new world where you have to start from scratch.”

This experience also allowed Pereira to gain a deeper understanding of their mother's perspective.

They learned to let go of certain expectations, finding understanding and support through other relationships instead. Friends became what Pereira calls their “chosen family,” walking alongside them through every phase of life. Pereira also rejects the idea that identity must remain static. “Who knows what someone will look like next year? Or ten years from now?” While Pereira says they don’t expect their identity to change again, they leave room for whatever the future might bring.

“By letting go of those expectations, I found my 「chosen family」—friends who truly reflect who I am and hold space for me at every stage.”

A Soulmate, Their Chosen Family

A JOY FRIEND, NOT A BOYFRIEND

Beyond gender identity, Pereira is also reframing the concept of intimacy.

Pereira’s partner, Wayne, is a musician and singer. The two connected online, striking up a conversation on a Sunday and going on their first date by Thursday of the following week.About a month into dating, Wayne asked if Pereira would be his "joyfriend" rather than his "boyfriend," honoring the fact that Pereira is not a boy.Pereira said yes.

I also love how attentive he is, and how silly he can be.

Pereira emphasizes that keeping the relationship healthy takes sustained effort and commitment from both sides.

"We are both so grateful for this relationship," Pereira said. "We hold space for each other, and we learn from one another."

05

STANDING TALLER

The person looking became the person seen.

In their youth, Pereira looked to film characters, queer artists, writers, and performers to find a sense of identity. These figures showed them that queer identity can take many forms and be expressed in endless ways.

A film character. A queer artist. A writer. A performer. Someone who made them think:

「Maybe there are other ways to live.」

Later, Pereira began sharing their own story. Pereira even delivered a TED Talk while balancing atop stilts. After the presentation, audience members approached them to share how deeply their story had resonated with their own lives. Those reactions prompted Pereira to realize that they, too, could serve as a source of inspiration for others.

People who heard the talk told them that something in the story felt familiar. That response stayed with Pereira.
